Below is a high-level roadmap for a front-end–only, “storytelling dashboard” that helps users explore the future of the French electrical system (2021–2025, then 2030 scenarios). The goal is to bring clarity, interactivity, and a narrative structure to the data. While the specific implementation details can vary, here are the best-practice considerations and steps.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Narrative Flow & Storytelling
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Define Key Messages: Identify the main points you want to convey—e.g., how projected CO2 emissions evolve, how the share of renewables compares across scenarios, or how nuclear energy remains a cornerstone of the French electrical mix.  
• Organize the Dashboard into Sections:  
  1) Introduction: Summarize the dataset and its purpose.  
  2) Historical/Current Snapshot (2021–2025): Show how the system evolves over the near term.  
  3) Future Outlook (2030 Scenarios): Present the two different scenario pathways.  
  4) Conclusions or Recommendations: Summarize key insights discovered.  

• Progressive Reveal: Use sections or slides (e.g., with an HTML-based stepper or card layout) that let users move through the story, focusing them on the data points that matter most.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. Data Sources & Handling Large Datasets
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• API Endpoint:  
  https://odre.opendatasoft.com/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets/bilan-previsionnel-electrique-2021-bilans-energetiques0/records  

• Dealing with Big Data in the Browser:  
  – Pagination or Lazy Loading: If the API supports pagination, request data in smaller chunks and combine or display them progressively.  
  – Selective Retrieval: Query only necessary columns or filters (e.g., by year, scenario). This reduces payload size.  
  – Client-Side Aggregation: Use JavaScript (e.g., D3 or a utility library like Lodash) to transform and group data once it’s loaded.  

• Data Columns (Likely examples from this dataset):  
  – year: Numeric indicator of the study year (e.g., 2021, 2025, 2030).  
  – scenario: Scenario label (e.g., “Scénario Commun”, “Scénario A”, “Scénario B”).  
  – emission_co2: Estimated CO2 emissions of the power system.  
  – share_renewables: Share of renewable energy in total production.  
  – share_nuclear: Share of nuclear energy in total production.  
  – energy_balance_info: This could be a high-level quantitative measure of supply/demand.  

Make sure you confirm the actual column names by inspecting the API’s JSON response.  

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. Data Transformations & Preprocessing
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Before rendering visualizations, transform the data into approachable formats:  

• Filtering:  
  – Filter by year range (2021–2025 or 2030).  
  – Filter by scenario (common scenario for the 2021–2025 horizon, two scenarios in 2030).  

• Grouping:  
  – Summarize CO2 emissions per year.  
  – Summarize share of renewables and nuclear per year/scenario.  

• Cumulative or Time Series Calculations:  
  – If needed, calculate yearly changes, average growth in renewables, or the difference in shares between the two 2030 scenarios.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. Visual Storytelling with Charts & Maps
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1) Time Series for CO2 Emissions  
   • Use a line chart to show CO2 emissions from 2021 to 2025, then extend to 2030 for each scenario.  
   • Highlight scenario differences at 2030 with color-coded lines or visually distinct markers.  

2) Stacked or Grouped Bar Chart for Share of Renewables vs. Nuclear  
   • For each year, show how renewables and nuclear split the total production share.  
   • Interactive tooltips can show exact percentages.  

3) Scenario Comparison View  
   • A toggle or dropdown to switch between the 2030 Scenario A and Scenario B.  
   • A side-by-side comparison chart or “before-and-after” slider could emphasize differences in CO2 emissions, renewables, and nuclear share.

4) Map (Optional)  
   • If the dataset includes any regional breakdown, an interactive map can display region-level differences.  
   • Hover tooltips on regions or clickable regions could filter charts by region.  

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. Interactivity & User Experience
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Dynamic Filtering & Searches: Allow filtering by year, scenario, or other attributes.  
• Hover Tooltips: On each chart, tooltips reinforce key data points (e.g., "In 2030 (Scenario A), renewables = X%, nuclear = Y%, CO2 = Z Mt").  
• Animated Transitions: When changing filters or moving between story sections, use smooth transitions to keep the viewer oriented.  
• Responsive Layout: Ensure the dashboard layout (cards, charts, legends) works well across desktop, tablets, and mobile.  

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. Front-End Tech Stack
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• HTML/CSS/JavaScript:  
  – Base technologies for building a fully front-end solution.  
• Chart Libraries:  
  – D3.js (powerful, flexible for custom visuals).  
  – Chart.js or Recharts (simpler charting out-of-the-box).  
• Map Libraries (optional):  
  – Leaflet.js or Mapbox.js for interactive maps if regional data is relevant.  
• Data Fetch & Handling:  
  – Use native Fetch API or axios to call the Opendatasoft API.  
  – Implement pagination or dynamic loading if dataset is large.  
  – Possibly use local caching to store the entire dataset in memory for faster transitions and comparisons once loaded.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. Bringing It All Together
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Storytelling Flow Example:  
  1) A high-level introduction card describing the dataset and the question: “How will the French electrical mix evolve from 2021 to 2030?”  
  2) A time-series chart of CO2 emissions for each year from 2021–2025. Let users hover or click for details.  
  3) A pivot to 2030 scenarios, with a scenario selection toggle. Show a combined line chart or two lines that branch out from 2025 to highlight the scenario differences (Scenario A vs. Scenario B).  
  4) A bar chart of the share of renewables vs nuclear in each scenario. Possibly animate or highlight how the percentages shift.  
  5) Provide interpretive text that explains the implications (e.g., “Scenario A envisions a 10% lower nuclear share, but slightly higher CO2 emissions than Scenario B”).  
  6) Concluding slide summarizing the key takeaways and linking to the official RTE “Bilan Prévisionnel 2021” for further reading.  

By combining these elements—clear story sections, well-chosen visualizations, and interactive features—you can create a compelling “dashboard storytelling” experience. The user learns not only about raw numbers but also about their meaning, trends, and policy implications in an engaging, front-end–only environment.
