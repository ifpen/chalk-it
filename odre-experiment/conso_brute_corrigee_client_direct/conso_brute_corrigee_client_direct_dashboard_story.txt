Below is a conceptual roadmap for designing a dashboard that tells a clear story around “conso_brute_corrigee_client_direct” using only front-end technologies. The goal is to build an engaging, interactive experience that handles large-scale data from the real API endpoint while focusing on clarity and storytelling.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Understanding the Dataset and Its Story
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Dataset Description:  
  - This dataset provides monthly national electricity consumption (in MWh) in France, including both brute consumption (conso_brute) and corrected consumption (conso_corrigee).  
  - It explicitly contains consumption from RTE’s directly connected clients (client_direct).  
• Time Span:  
  - Typically, data covers multiple consecutive years or months (depending on how the dataset is structured).  
• Possible Fields (as gleaned from the description & naming patterns):  
  - date (or year, month): The date or period of the data point.  
  - conso_brute: The raw electricity consumption in MWh.  
  - conso_corrigee: The weather-adjusted or otherwise corrected consumption in MWh.  
  - client_direct: Consumption values of directly connected network clients.  
  - Possibly additional fields like region codes, if available.  

Your storytelling can revolve around:  
• How raw consumption compares to corrected consumption over time.  
• Trends or patterns in direct client consumption vs. the total consumption.  
• Seasonal or monthly variations (e.g., winter vs. summer).  
• Growth or decline over different years.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. Dealing with Big Data on the Front-End
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Even though your sample might display only 20 rows, the real dataset can contain thousands of records. To ensure performance and interactivity:  
• Use Pagination or Incremental Loading:  
  - Make requests in pages (e.g., 1000 records at a time) from the official endpoint:  
    “https://odre.opendatasoft.com/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets/conso_brute_corrigee_client_direct/records”  
  - Load new data only when needed (e.g., on scrolling or by date range selection).  
• Caching and Data Management:  
  - Store retrieved data in memory or in IndexedDB/localStorage (if feasible) for quick local access.  
  - Consider data aggregation on the fly (e.g., summing monthly data if your storytelling focuses on annual trends).  
• Efficient Libraries for Visualization:  
  - Tools like D3.js, ECharts, Highcharts, or Chart.js can handle dynamic updates and larger datasets on the client side if used thoughtfully.  
  - Libraries such as Crossfilter and dc.js can help with synchronized filtering across multiple charts (if you want advanced interactions).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. Structuring the Dashboard for Clarity & Engagement
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A storytelling approach ensures that the user’s journey is guided by clear insights rather than random data dumps. Here’s a suggested layout:

A. Overview / “Hero” Section  
• Big Numeric Highlights:  
  - Show total consumption or the year-over-year growth rate.  
  - Quick gauge or big text blocks for the most recent monthly consumption.  
• Context/Trends:  
  - A line chart or area chart comparing conso_brute vs. conso_corrigee over time.  
  - This effectively tells the story of how raw consumption is corrected and why that matters.

B. Drill-Down / Interactive Components  
1) Time-Series Exploration:  
   - An interactive timeline (e.g., a combined bar / line chart) allows filtering by date range.  
   - Hover tooltips display monthly conso_brute, conso_corrigee, and client_direct details.  

2) Regional/Map View (if regional data is available):  
   - Interactive map displaying consumption by region or zone.  
   - Clicking on a region updates charts to show that region’s monthly trend.  

3) Comparison Panels:  
   - A side-by-side chart or switch toggle to see conso_brute vs. conso_corrigee or to display them layered for quick comparison.  

C. Key Insights / Explanations  
• Provide short narrative text boxes or tooltips explaining:  
  - The difference between brute and corrected consumption.  
  - Why client_direct consumption is meaningful (e.g., large-scale industrial usage).  
  - Notable historical events or patterns (holidays, weather anomalies).

D. Filters and Interactions  
• Date Range Filter:  
  - Let users choose a range (yearly or monthly).  
• Data Type Filter:  
  - Toggle between showing absolute MWh or percentage changes.  
• Comparison Mode:  
  - Compare conso_brute vs. conso_corrigee or vs. client_direct consumption in one chart.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. Implementation Tips (Front-End Only)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Fetching Data:  
  - Use fetch or Axios to call the ODS (OpenDataSoft) REST endpoint.  
  - The endpoint’s query parameters often allow for specifying date ranges, sorting, or limit/offset for pagination.  

• Data Transformation:  
  - Convert date strings into Date objects for easier aggregation (e.g., grouping by Month or Quarter).  
  - Summarize or average data if you need annual totals or monthly means for a line chart.  
  - Create derived metrics (e.g., daily average, consumed MWh per day, or transformations removing outliers).

• Visualization Tools & Flow:  
  - For big data, libraries like D3 with Crossfilter can help partition and filter data in near real-time.  
  - For simpler setups with smaller chunks, Chart.js or Highcharts is quick to configure.  
  - Keep the design approach modular: Data fetching and transformation in one file/module, visual rendering in another.

• Storytelling Components:  
  - Introduce brief text blocks or pop-ups that guide the user through the visuals.  
  - Color-coding and labeling are crucial to clarity.  
  - Provide legends, data disclaimers, and short data explanations.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. Tying It All Together
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
When the user lands on your dashboard, they should instantly see:  
1) A clear “headline” chart or set of key figures summarizing the major trend of electricity consumption.  
2) An interactive timeline or map to explore patterns.  
3) Well-placed narrative elements (text or tooltips) explaining the significance of conso_brute vs. conso_corrigee and how direct clients fit in.  

As they drill down, the interface seamlessly fetches additional data from the real endpoint, ensuring the entire time series (possibly spanning years) can be viewed without performance bottlenecks. The user leaves the dashboard with a clear understanding of overall consumption trends and the real-world context of the data.

This combination of strong visual design, modular data loading, and contextual explanations ensures a dashboard that engages the audience—whether they are data enthusiasts or stakeholders looking for quick insights into France’s electricity consumption.
