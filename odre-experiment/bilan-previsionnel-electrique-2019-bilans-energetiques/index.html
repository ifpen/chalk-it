Bilan Prévisionnel Électrique 2019 (Cas de Base)

This interactive dashboard highlights key forecasts from RTE’s “Bilan Prévisionnel Électrique 2019” dataset, focusing on six forecast years between 2020 and 2026. In particular, it explores:

  • Overall electricity production trends.
  • CO₂ emissions over time.
  • The evolving shares of nuclear vs. renewable energy.

Scroll down to follow the story, or use the navigation links above to jump to a specific section. All data is fetched live from the open data platform (ODRE) at: odre.opendatasoft.com.

1) Overview: Total Forecasted Production

Below is the total forecasted electricity production (TWh) for each available year in the base scenario. Though the dataset includes various production sources (nuclear, renewables, fossil fuels), this chart provides a high-level picture of how total generation evolves.

Observing overall production is a great starting point to see if electricity output is growing, stagnating, or decreasing, and it serves as a baseline for deeper dives into emissions and energy mixes.

2) CO₂ Emissions Focus

Decarbonization is a key pillar of energy policy. The bar chart below visualizes forecasted CO₂ emissions (million metric tons). This metric is important for understanding the environmental impact of electricity generation.

France’s reliance on nuclear energy often keeps emissions lower than in countries heavily dependent on coal or gas. However, natural gas and other thermal sources still contribute to the emissions trajectory over time.

3) Nuclear vs. Renewables

This final chart shows the shares of nuclear and renewable energy in the total electricity mix (reported by the dataset as fractional contributions). Hover over each data point to see exact values.

Renewable sources include solar, wind, hydro, bioenergies, and (potentially) marine energies. As you can see, France’s famous nuclear fleet is expected to remain a significant portion of the mix, while renewables show notable growth in most scenarios.

Key Takeaways & Next Steps

France’s electricity outlook through the mid-2020s, according to this dataset, shows a stable or slightly declining total production, modest CO₂ emissions, a strong nuclear base, and an expanding renewables sector. While nuclear remains prominent, the trajectory suggests a continued push toward renewables in the coming years.

Use the dashboard interactivity (tooltips, year-by-year data) to further explore the details. For more context, visit the official ODRE portal for additional scenarios and underlying assumptions.
