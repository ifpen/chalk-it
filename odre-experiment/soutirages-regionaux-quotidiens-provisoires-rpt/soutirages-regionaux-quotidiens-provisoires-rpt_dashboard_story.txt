Below is an outline of how you might design a purely front-end, “storytelling” dashboard for the “soutirages-regionaux-quotidiens-provisoires-rpt” dataset. The goal is to present insights (daily/half-hourly electricity consumption) in a clear, interactive, and engaging way—all while handling large volumes of data beyond the small sample.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1) DATA INGESTION & HANDLING BIG DATA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
• Use the official API endpoint:
  https://odre.opendatasoft.com/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets/soutirages-regionaux-quotidiens-provisoires-rpt/records
  
• Data Volume & Pagination:
  – The dataset can grow very large over time (with daily granularity, half-hour intervals, multiple regions, etc.).
  – Take advantage of server-side pagination or filtering provided by the API. For instance, you can declare a page size (e.g., 50 or 100 rows at a time) and handle “Load more” functionality in the front-end.
  – Consider dynamic loading (fetching only data for the date range or the regions in view). This helps avoid a single massive dataset load.

• Data Fields (typical fields in each record):
  – date: The day of measurement.  
  – half_hour: Half-hour slot within that day.  
  – region_name: Administrative region (e.g., Île-de-France, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, etc.).  
  – sector_label: The sector of activity (e.g., consumer, producer, industrial, etc.).  
  – tension: Voltage level of the connection (e.g., HTB, etc.).  
  – power_mw: The power at that half-hour (in MW).  
  – daily_energy_mwh: The total daily energy in MWh (summing or aggregating half-hours).

Depending on the API structure, you might see these in a “fields” object within each “record” item. Adjust naming in your code accordingly.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
2) DATA TRANSFORMATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
• Aggregate by Day & Region:
  – Summarize half-hour records to get daily consumption per region (or sector).  
  – This helps quickly visualize total daily consumption trends without scanning all half-hours.

• Aggregate by Half-hour over Time:
  – Useful for identifying peak vs. off-peak usage.  
  – For instance, create a 24-hour profile (48 x half-hours in the day) and see how usage changes over a typical day.

• Grouping & Filtering:
  – By sector: Compare patterns in industrial vs. residential consumption.  
  – By region: Spot regional consumption differences.  
  – By tension: Possibly highlight which voltage levels see the most usage.

• Handle Missing or Masked Values:
  – Because certain lines are masked to protect confidentiality, you may have incomplete data.  
  – Build logic to ignore or mark these gaps so they don’t skew visualizations (or display them as “No Data”).

3) STORYTELLING DASHBOARD DESIGN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A storytelling dashboard guides the audience from high-level insights down to detailed views. Here is a recommended layout:

A) Overview Section (High-Level Narrative)
  – Show total daily consumption (across all regions) for the last week or month in a line or area chart.  
  – Provide key metrics:  
    1) Highest daily consumption  
    2) Trend (increasing/decreasing vs. last period)  
    3) Peak day or half-hour  

B) Regional Comparison (Geographic + Chart)
  – Map Visualization:
    • Color-code regions by total daily consumption or average half-hour load.  
    • Hover interactions: Tooltips to show region name, daily total, etc.  
  – Bar/Stacked Bar Chart (to the side):
    • X-axis: Regions  
    • Y-axis: MW or MWh  
    • Potentially stack by sector or tension to see composition.

C) Temporal Drill-Down
  – A time-based chart (line or bar):  
    • X-axis: Time (dates or half-hour slots).  
    • Y-axis: MW consumption.  
    • Dropdown/Filters to choose region(s) or sector(s).  
  – This allows the user to see daily or half-hourly patterns.  
  – Add a brush/pan feature (a smaller timeline below the main chart) for zooming in on specific date ranges while still elegantly handling large date spans.

D) Sector Analysis
  – Pie/Donut Chart (daily or date-range-based):
    • Show the proportion of consumption by each sector_label (industrial, residential, etc.).  
  – Interactive legend: click on a sector to isolate it in the other charts.  

E) Tension-Level Insights
  – Use a stacked or grouped bar chart to compare usage across tension categories.  
  – Possibly show how high-voltage and low-voltage usage differ over time or by region.

4) FRONT-END TECHNOLOGIES & INTERACTIVITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
• Framework/Library Options:
  – React, Vue, or Angular for the overall UI.  
  – Chart Libraries: D3.js, Chart.js, Recharts (React), or ECharts.  
  – Map Libraries: Leaflet, Mapbox GL JS, or a specialized D3 geo-map approach.  

• Best Practices:
  – Keep charts minimal, focusing on clear axes, labels, and tooltips.  
  – Add color-coding with a consistent palette (e.g., each sector gets a color).  
  – Provide chart legends and make them interactive (click to toggle sectors/regions).  
  – Use clear, large fonts for axes/titles to highlight the story.  

• Performance Considerations:
  – Implement on-demand “drill-down” fetching. Only load half-hour data if a user clicks on a day or region.  
  – Cache data in local state or local storage to minimize repeated API calls.  
  – Use lazy-loading or code-splitting in large frameworks to optimize the dashboard load time.

5) PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER – STORY FLOW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1) Start with a “Today’s Snapshot”: 
   – Show today’s total consumption + a quick historical comparison (up/down vs. average).  
2) Zoom Out and Compare Regions:
   – Interactive map or bar chart highlighting the highest vs. lowest consumption regions.  
3) Let the User “Drill Down”: 
   – They can pick a region on the map or select a date range in a time series widget.  
   – The half-hourly breakdown chart updates accordingly.  
4) Sector Breakdown:
   – Provide a breakdown of how industrial vs. residential vs. other sectors consume power.  
   – Show anomalies or peaks with highlights (e.g., “Peak usage at 6:00 PM in industrial sector”).  
5) Voltage-Level Analysis (Optional extra layer):
   – If relevant to your story, highlight how usage at different voltage levels compares.

Each step leads the user deeper into the data, providing them with both the “big picture” and the ability to explore specific questions. Throughout, maintain an approachable tone in textual annotations: “In Île-de-France, consumption peaks in the morning, driven by industrial start-ups,” etc.
