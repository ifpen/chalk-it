Below is an outline of how you can design a front-end–only, “story-driven” data visualization dashboard for the “monitoring-capacites-echanges-nib” dataset, with an emphasis on clarity, interactivity, and scalability.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Frame Your Narrative: From Regulation to Real Data
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Start your dashboard by explaining the context:  
  – The Clean Energy Package 70% target: Why this threshold exists and its implications.  
  – The role of RTE and CRE in publishing these cross-border capacity figures.  
  – The significance of monitoring capacity from a reliability and cross-border trade perspective.  

• Story overview:  
  – Explain that each data record pertains to a specific critical network element (CNE) at a given timestamp.  
  – Clarify that “NIB” covers the capacity calculations relevant to the France–Italy interconnection.  

This opening sets the stage so users understand why these numbers matter before they dive into charts.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. Accessing and Handling “Big Data”
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Even though you only have sample JSON of up to 20 records, you should plan for a large dataset. The official API endpoint is:  
https://odre.opendatasoft.com/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets/monitoring-capacites-echanges-nib/records

Key considerations:  
• Pagination and Filtering:  
  – Since the Opendatasoft API may provide paginated data, retrieve it in chunks (e.g., 100/500 rows at a time) as needed.  
  – Use front-end pagination or virtual scrolling for tables or lists of timestamps/CNEs, so you don’t load everything at once.  

• Asynchronous AJAX/Fetch calls:  
  – Implement lazy loading for charts (load data only when the user navigates to that specific part of the dashboard).  
  – Cache results on the client side if the user is likely to revisit the filtered view (e.g., daily or monthly aggregations).  

• Data Transformation:  
  – Pre-aggregate (on the fly) large or detailed data into aggregated metrics (e.g., daily average capacity usage vs. the 70% threshold) for more fluid chart performance.  
  – Employ time-series grouping (by day, week, or month) if the dataset is in half-year increments but contains frequent timestamps.  

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. Identifying Data Columns & Transformations
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Though the exact schema can vary, typical columns for this dataset might include:  
• timestamp (date/time)  
• critical_network_element (string)  
• region (string) – e.g., NIB, SWE, CWE/CORE  
• available_capacity (numeric) – the capacity made available for cross-border exchanges  
• total_capacity (numeric) – the total theoretical capacity of that element  
• capacity_utilization (numeric or derived) – e.g., available_capacity / total_capacity × 100  

Useful transformations for storytelling:  
• Capacity Factor: capacity_utilization can be visualized as a percentage over time.  
• Threshold Comparison: highlight when capacity_utilization < 70% or ≥ 70%.  
• Regional Aggregation: group lines or bars by region (when comparing multiple borders).  
• Distribution of “Critical Network Elements”: how many elements are meeting the threshold across a date range?  

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. Designing the Visualization Flow
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Here’s a suggested flow for a single-page dashboard with multiple interactive components:

(A) Overview/Key Figures  
• Large numeric tiles (or summary cards) at the top:  
  – Average % capacity made available in the selected half-year.  
  – Number of timestamps that meet or exceed 70%.  
  – Number of critical network elements monitored.  

(B) Time-Series Chart  
• Line or area chart:  
  – x-axis: Date/Time (timestamp).  
  – y-axis: capacity_utilization (%).  
  – Reference line at 70% to show the regulatory threshold.  
• Interactivity:  
  – Hover tooltips showing exact capacity_utilization, timestamp, region/CNE.  
  – Brushing or zooming to isolate specific date ranges.  

(C) CNE Detail Table with Filters  
• A table listing each critical_network_element, with columns for:  
  – Region, available_capacity, total_capacity, capacity_utilization, timestamp.  
• Filters:  
  – Region drop-down (NIB, SWE, CWE, etc.).  
  – Date range selector.  
  – Capacity threshold (e.g., only show elements below/above 70%).  
• Pagination to handle big data.  

(D) Geographic/Border Map (optional if geocoordinates are available or if region-level is enough)  
• Display a simplified map highlighting the cross-border lines relevant to France–Italy (NIB).  
• Use color-coding to show how often or how recently lines have fallen below the threshold.  

(E) Trend Breakdown / “Story Highlights” Section  
• A stepped narrative that explains, for instance:  
  – “In Q1, 85% of NIB capacities exceeded the 70% threshold on average. But from mid-April to May, the threshold was not met as often, likely reflecting increased demand or planned maintenance.”  
• This ties into the line chart, with clickable text or markers that highlight relevant date ranges.  

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. Ensuring Clarity & Interactivity
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Use color-coded logic to draw immediate attention to sub-70% capacity points.  
• Provide interactive legends so users can toggle on/off different critical network elements or regions.  
• Incorporate simple text explanations or tooltips to decode abbreviations (e.g., “NIB: Northern Italy Border region”).  
• Keep tooltips uncluttered (focus on a few key metrics rather than listing all columns).  

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. Technical Stack (Front-End Only)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Although you can do this all with vanilla JavaScript, here are some suggestions for libraries that facilitate large data handling and storytelling:

• Data Fetching & State Management:  
  – If using React, you can manage global state with Redux or React Query to handle data fetching/pagination.  
  – In Vue or Angular, build a service to fetch data and keep it cached across components.

• Charting Libraries:  
  – D3.js: Highly customizable, ideal for unique storytelling and complex interactions.  
  – Chart.js or Apache ECharts: Simpler to set up, good for standard line/bar charts with tooltips and legends.  
  – Plotly.js: Built-in interactive features (zoom, pan, tooltips) out of the box.  

• Mapping:  
  – Leaflet.js or Mapbox GL JS if you have any geographical details.  
  – Alternatively, a simple D3-based map for cross-border lines if you have shapefiles or geoJSON data.  

• Performance Optimization for Big Data:  
  – Use canvas-based rendering (e.g., Chart.js or ECharts in canvas mode) rather than SVG if you expect tens of thousands of points.  
  – Dynamically aggregate or downsample your data by time intervals to avoid over-plotting.  

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. Putting It All Together
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Intro Section:  
  – State the regulation (70% threshold), link to any official documentation, and define the scope (France–Italy border, half-year increments).  

• Main Interactive Charts:  
  – Display your primary time-series chart with capacity_utilization.  
  – Let users hover to see exact values and highlight regulatory compliance or shortfalls.  

• Drill-Down Views:  
  – A table that updates based on user-selected date range or threshold filter.  
  – A map or region-based graphic if feasible.  

• Storytelling Narrative:  
  – Provide textual “chapters” or bullet points next to or below the charts that summarize key insights (e.g., “In the first half of 2020, the capacity often dropped below 70% on weekdays.”).  
  – Offer an option to “drill into outliers” or “explore time range of interest.”  

• Conclusion & Next Steps:  
  – Summarize findings: how often does the NIB region meet the 70% target?  
  – Invite users to explore a broader timeline, or cross-compare with other regions (SWE, CWE/CORE).

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

• Allow CSV/Excel export of filtered views for deeper offline analysis.  
• Responsive design for mobile or smaller screens (split charts into vertical stacking on smaller viewports).
